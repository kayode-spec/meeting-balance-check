Team process prototype

Meeting Balance Check

Paste a team meeting transcript to see who is carrying the conversation, who is being left out, and what the team leader and each member can do to make participation more even.

How to use it

  1. Get your transcript. Zoom: Recordings → download the audio transcript (.vtt). Otter or Teams: export as .txt or .vtt. A plain Name: what they said format also works.
  2. Paste it below or upload the file. No transcript yet? Click Load sample to try it.
  3. List your team members (optional) so anyone who never spoke still shows up in the results. This also improves accuracy: only listed names count as speakers, and first-name/full-name variants are merged.
  4. Add your team charter norms (optional), such as a minimum or maximum share of the conversation, to see who met them.
  5. Click Analyze. Review the team stats, the "who responds to whom" map, the guidance for the leader, and the note for each member.
  6. Share it. Use Copy summary to paste the results into your team chat or a meeting debrief.

Privacy: everything runs in your browser. Your transcript is never uploaded or stored anywhere.

    Research behind the design
    • Woolley, Chabris, Pentland, Hashmi & Malone (2010). Evidence for a collective intelligence factor in the performance of human groups. Science. Teams with more equal turn-taking performed better across tasks.
    • Pentland (2012). The new science of building great teams. Harvard Business Review. High-performing teams have even, energetic exchanges and short contributions rather than monologues.
    • Edmondson (1999). Psychological safety and learning behavior in work teams. Administrative Science Quarterly. Members speak up when they believe it is safe to do so.
    • Hackman (2002). Leading Teams. Clear norms of conduct, like those in a team charter, are part of the enabling structure effective teams need.
    • Nembhard & Edmondson (2006). Leader inclusiveness: leaders who explicitly invite input increase psychological safety and participation.
